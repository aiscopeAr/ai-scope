Executive summary

The headline number — 559 pages, 750 impressions, 8 clicks — looks like a content-quality problem. It mostly isn't. It's a distribution and crawl-graph problem: for eight weeks, most of the site's own content had no path for Google or a reader to reach it, and the pages that were reachable were frequently mis-labeled, mis-categorized, or duplicated. Several of these were structural bugs, not strategy gaps — fixed this week, verified against production, not yet reflected in the GSC numbers above.

1

Internal link graph was broken, not thin

203 of 227 reviews had zero inbound link path from any page on the site — /reviews and /category/[slug] capped at 24 items with no pagination link at all; /prompts had pagination, but as a JS onClick handler Googlebot can't follow. Fixed Jul 21 — real <a href> pagination now ships on all three.

Fixed, unverified in GSC
2

92% of articles sat in one mislabeled category

The auto-publish cron defaulted every article to the DB's first category row regardless of topic. 210 of 227 reviews landed in "companies," and 7 of 10 categories sat empty — flattening "related articles" into effectively random picks and leaving whole topic hubs with no content. Fixed Jul 21; backfilled 152 existing reviews.

Fixed Jul 21
3

8 of 25 RSS sources never produced a single article

A too-strict content-length filter and a parser bug that silently dropped every Atom-format feed's URL (Product Hunt, Simon Willison) meant a third of the news pipeline was dead — and looked healthy, because the sync timestamp updates whether or not anything was written. Fixed Jul 22; verified against the real production DB.

Fixed Jul 22
4

Cache directives were no-ops for six months

export const revalidate = N only caches fetch() calls — this codebase reads exclusively through Prisma. Every listing page was hitting Postgres on every request regardless of the config. Fixed Jul 21 with unstable_cache and tag-based invalidation on publish.

Fixed Jul 21
5

Domain has near-zero backlink profile and zero AI-crawler visibility

robots.txt blocks GPTBot, CCBot, and Claude-Web outright. For a site whose subject is AI, that forecloses citation by the assistants most likely to reference an AI-news source. No linkable assets exist yet to earn backlinks even if crawled.

Open — see §7
→

What this means for the roadmap: the structural fixes are largely done. The next 90 days are about giving Google and readers a reason to return — publish-volume discipline, topical depth in the categories that are now populated correctly, and the first linkable assets. Section 10 lays out the sequence.

02

Search performance analysis

Why does a 559-page site have 750 impressions and 8 clicks? Ranked by how much of the gap each bottleneck actually explains.

1

Indexing bottleneck — largest single factor

GSC showed 140 "not indexed" pages against only 53 indexed as of late June; the site's sitemap was stuck at 142 URLs (vs. the 658 that exist) until it was manually resubmitted Jul 2. A site can't rank pages Google hasn't chosen to index, and it can't choose to index pages it can't reach via crawl or sitemap. Both were broken simultaneously.

Impact: severe
2

Zero topical authority — a brand-new domain

Every metric here reflects a domain with effectively no trust signal yet: no backlinks, no brand search volume beyond the literal string "lumiq," average position ~18–26 even on pages with real content. This is expected for a domain at this age and is the single hardest bottleneck to fix quickly — it responds to time + authority-building (§7), not code.

Impact: severe
3

Keyword targeting was accidentally right, execution was weak

The queries that do land clicks — "gemini in arabic," "gemini بالعربي" — show real demand for AI-tool content in Arabic. But gemini and dalle-3's own pages had seoTitle: null, falling back to a generic template that didn't target those exact phrases. Fixed Jul 21 for 10 tools total.

Impact: moderate
4

AI-generated content quality — real, but narrower than it looks

Most published content is coherent, structured, and topically correct (verified by reading actual generated articles end-to-end this week). The measurable quality problem is narrower: duplicate topics from an editorial-calendar-blind generator (the ChatGPT pair), and 10 flagship tool pages (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini among them) that never got past a 400-character auto-description. Both are fixable without touching the generation pipeline's core quality.

Impact: moderate
5

Backlinks — effectively zero, compounding #2

No linkable asset exists yet (no original data, no free tool, no citable ranking). This is a downstream symptom of a young site with no distribution strategy beyond organic search, not a separate technical defect.

Impact: moderate, compounds #2
6

Poor internal linking — largely resolved this week

Covered in §1. Listed here for completeness in the ranking; no longer an open bottleneck as of Jul 21–22, pending re-crawl.

Resolved
7

Search-intent mismatch — minor, isolated

No systemic mismatch found. The one clear case is the ChatGPT duplicate (§1) — two different intents ("what is X" vs. "how to use X") accidentally executed as two competing pages instead of one comprehensive one.

Impact: minor

Increase

Tool reviews with real depth — 10 of the highest-intent pages on the site (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini among them) just went from a 400-character stub to a full review; this pattern should be the default for every new tool, not a backfill exception. Evergreen "what is / how to" guides — these already capture the only clicks the site has ("gemini in arabic"); double down deliberately instead of accidentally. Prompts — already the single best-performing page type by CTR (7.3% vs. site average ~1%) with the least competition; under-resourced relative to its return.

Decrease

Undifferentiated daily news clustering — 135 articles now correctly live under "ai-models," but many are generic "Model X released" rewrites competing directly with TechCrunch and VentureBeat's speed advantage, a race this site cannot and should not try to win. Redirect that generation capacity toward analysis and evergreen assets instead of chasing same-day news.

Build next

The empty categories are the map: research & papers, safety & ethics, policy & regulation, infrastructure — zero articles each, despite being real, searched topics with far less competition than "ai-models." A single well-linked pillar page per empty category, then 8–10 supporting articles, would give each one topical density it currently has none of.

6-month content roadmap

Month 1–2

Stop the bleeding, fix the highest-leverage pages

  • Full content pass on all 51 tool pages — no more null contentAr anywhere.
  • One pillar page per empty category (research, safety, policy, infrastructure) — 1,500+ words, internally linked from every related tool/review.
  • Audit the full 236-article corpus for duplicate topics beyond the ChatGPT pair already found.
Month 3–4

Build the evergreen backbone

  • 8–10 supporting articles per newly-seeded category, targeting long-tail "how to" and "what is" queries specifically.
  • Expand comparisons from 4 to 20+ — comparison pages carry the highest commercial intent and the clearest monetization path (affiliate).
  • Launch a weekly "state of AI in Arabic" roundup — a recurring, linkable, subscribable format.
Month 5–6

Scale programmatic + earn first links

  • Ship the use-case × industry matrix from §4 — the first real programmatic SEO surface.
  • Publish the first original data asset (see §7) as deliberate link bait.
  • Re-audit GSC: expect indexing to have caught up: target 300+ indexed pages, 500+ clicks/month.
04

Programmatic SEO strategy

559 → 10,000+ pages is a real ceiling for this niche, but only if each template has a genuine, non-interchangeable reason to exist. The schema already supports most of this — it's mostly unused capacity, not new infrastructure.

AI tool × use case

~900 pages
51 tools × ~18 use cases already defined in lib/tool-categories.ts. Template: "Best AI tools for {use case}" listing pages — /ai-tools/for/[usecase] already exists; the ceiling is tool count, not code.

Tool alternatives

~250 pages
"{Tool} alternatives" and "{Tool A} vs {Tool B}" — high commercial intent, direct affiliate-monetization fit. Comparison schema already exists (Comparison model); only 4 of a realistic ~250 pairs are built.

Industry × AI solution

~150 pages
"AI tools for {industry}" (healthcare, legal, finance — categories already exist in the schema). Needs one curated intro paragraph per industry plus a filtered tool grid — mostly templating, minimal new generation.

Prompt × tool

~1,500 pages
The prompt library already links prompts to tools (toolId FK). Systematic "Best {tool} prompts for {task}" pages are close to free given the existing 272-prompt base and its 7.3% CTR track record.

Tag hubs

54 pages, live
Shipped Jul 21 — 533 raw tags deduplicated (Arabic definite-article variants merged) down to 54 pages with real depth (3+ articles each). The pattern to replicate: never publish a hub thinner than 3 real items.

Tutorials (evergreen)

~200 pages
Already has a dedicated author persona ("team") and category. Currently only 11 published against a template that should be the site's highest-volume evergreen category, not its smallest.
⚠

The anti-spam constraint that makes or breaks this: every one of today's real problems — the duplicate ChatGPT pair, the 12 mis-tagged tools, the empty categories — came from generating pages without checking what already exists. Programmatic scale without a pre-publish duplicate/coverage check is exactly how a 10,000-page plan turns into a doorway-page penalty. The one piece of infrastructure this needs before scaling: a topic-coverage check against existing slugs/tags at generation time, not after.

07

Authority-building strategy

Zero backlinks today. These are the assets worth building specifically because this site can build them and a generic news rewrite can't.

Linkable assets to build

An "AI tools in Arabic — support tracker" — the site already flags arabicSupport per tool in its own schema; publishing this as a standalone, regularly-updated dataset is a citable resource nothing else in the niche has in Arabic. A "State of Arabic AI content" report, published quarterly, using the site's own GSC/traffic data as the hook.

Reconsider the AI-crawler block

robots.txt currently blocks GPTBot, CCBot, and Claude-Web. For most publishers that's the right call against training scraping — but it also means ChatGPT browsing and Claude's web search can never cite Lumiq as a source. Worth a deliberate choice, not a default: keep blocking training crawlers, consider allowing live-browsing agents specifically.

Digital PR & partnerships

MENA tech publication guest posts and co-citation swaps; Arabic-language developer communities (Discord/Telegram AI groups) as the first non-search distribution channel while search authority is still building; AI tool vendors directly — the site already reviews their tools, so a "verified review" partnership costs nothing to propose and gives vendors a reason to link back.

Technical implementation plan

Stack-specific — this is Next.js 16 on Vercel, Prisma/Postgres, OpenAI + Replicate for generation. Recommendations build on what's there, not a rip-and-replace.

Already correct, keep as-is

  • unstable_cache + tag-based invalidation, shipped Jul 21 — the right primitive for a Prisma-backed app on this Next.js version.
  • ISR via generateStaticParams on article pages — genuinely fast, verified directly (sub-second response times measured).
  • The review/tool/prompt schema is already relational enough to support the §4 programmatic templates without new tables.

Add next

  • A SourceRun-style outcome log (shipped Jul 22 for RSS ingestion) extended to every cron stage — cluster, process, publish — so a silently-broken stage is visible within a day, not a month.
  • A pre-publish duplicate-topic check (embedding similarity against existing slugs) before the programmatic scale-up in §4.
  • A scheduled newsletter-send job — the template/component exists; the cron doesn't yet.
